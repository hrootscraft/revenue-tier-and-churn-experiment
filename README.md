# Fee Tier Acceleration Experiment

**Can pricing transparency drive institutional trading volume without destroying margins?**

An end-to-end causal inference project simulating a real A/B test at a crypto institutional exchange. Tests whether showing clients their progress toward the next fee tier increases volume AND whether the resulting margin compression makes it worthwhile.

---

## The Business Problem

Coinbase Institutional charges fees in basis points (bps) on a tiered schedule. Think of it like bulk pricing: the more you trade, the cheaper each dollar of trading becomes:

| Tier         | Maker Fee | Taker Fee | You need to trade... |
| ------------ | --------- | --------- | -------------------- |
| Enterprise   | 2 bps     | 7 bps     | >$500M/quarter       |
| Professional | 4 bps     | 10 bps    | >$100M/quarter       |
| Standard     | 6 bps     | 16 bps    | >$25M/quarter        |
| Starter      | 10 bps    | 26 bps    | Below $25M           |

*Maker orders (limit orders that add liquidity) pay a lower fee than taker orders (market orders that remove liquidity). A client's effective rate is a blend based on their maker/taker mix.*

Many clients sit just below a tier boundary without knowing it. A client trading $90M/quarter at Standard tier pays a blended rate of ~12 bps (depending on their maker/taker mix). If they traded $101M, they'd move to Professional tier at ~7.6 bps blended, saving roughly $27K/quarter. But they don't know they're close.

**The tension:** If we show clients their progress and they trade more (good), some will cross into a cheaper tier (bad for margins). Does the volume gain outweigh the rate compression?

## Key Findings

The experiment reveals a non-obvious insight: **the optimal targeting depends on client proximity to the next tier.**

- **Clients 10-20% from the next tier** are the sweet spot — they trade more but mostly don't cross, generating +11% net revenue
- **Clients within 10%** generate minimal short-term revenue (+2%) but show 60% lower churn (the "golden handcuff" effect) — making it a retention investment
- **CUPED** reduces variance by ~50%, equivalent to doubling the sample size
- **Treatment effect decays ~2% per week**, suggesting a novelty component — naive annualization would overstate impact by ~40%
- **No evidence of cannibalization** — control volume remained stable

## Data Quality Guarantees

Every pipeline run validates:

- Revenue = MakerVolume × MakerRate + TakerVolume × TakerRate (exact, not approximate)
- Revenue decomposition terms sum to total ΔRevenue (mathematical identity)
- Tier assignments match volume thresholds (boundary conditions tested)
- No treatment leakage into pre-period data
- Assignment balanced within all strata
- No duplicate clients (catches JOIN bugs)
